Nothing on this page is financial advice. It's how I think, not what I hold.

I studied Mathematics with Computing, so I came to markets expecting them to
reward the thing I was trained in: modelling. They partly do, and the part that
doesn't is the more interesting half.

## What the maths actually gives you

Three things, and they're smaller than they sound.

The first is a working sense of distributions — that an average tells you very
little on its own, that variance is the part that decides whether you're still
in the game next year, and that outcomes with fat tails don't behave like the
tidy bell curve in a textbook.

The second is compounding, understood properly rather than quoted. The gap
between 6% and 8% over thirty years is not a 2% difference, and neither is the
gap between a 30% drawdown and a 50% one — you need 43% to recover from the
first and 100% from the second.

The third is scepticism about backtests. If you've ever fit a model to data you
already had, you know exactly how easy it is to find a rule that would have
worked and won't.

## What it doesn't give you

Everything else. A model tells you what's reasonable given assumptions; it says
nothing about whether you'll actually hold the position when it's down 30% and
the reasoning still looks sound. That's a temperament problem wearing a maths
costume, and no amount of modelling solves it.

The other thing the degree didn't prepare me for is how much of investing is
just not doing things. Most of the value is in the decisions you decline to
make.

## How I approach it

- **Boring by default.** Broad, low-cost, regular contributions, held. If a
  position needs constant attention it's a job, not an investment.
- **Position sizing before conviction.** Being right is optional; surviving
  being wrong isn't.
- **Write the thesis down first.** If I can't state in two sentences why I'm
  buying and what would prove me wrong, that's the signal to skip it.
- **Treat fees and taxes as the one guaranteed return.** They're the only
  numbers on the page that are certain.
